The approach

One sentence, one loop, one artefact. If any part of the build cannot be traced back to these three, it does not get built this week.

The sentence you open and close with

“Every platform in e-waste onboards a business. We onboard the person below them — and going formal pays a Nagpur collector more the same day, because the handover record we produce is worth money to the recycler and we share that value back at the point of sale.”

Say it in the first thirty seconds. Say it again at the end. Every slide in between is evidence for one clause of it.

Rename the product tonight

Kabadiwalla Connect is a real Chennai social enterprise, operating since 2014 with World Economic Forum and Shell backing, and The Kabadiwala in Bhopal is a second collision. Presenting under either name to a panel is an unforced error that costs you nothing to avoid. Keep the problem-statement title in the submission form; give the product its own name.

Bhaav is the suggestion — it is the exact word a collector uses for the rate they are not told, it reads in Marathi and Hindi, and it names the thing you actually deliver. Tolmol (weigh-and-price) is the alternative. Pick one in five minutes and move on; the name is not worth an hour.

The loop — this is the entire product

Six screens, one path, no branches. A collector with a ₹6,000 phone, no ledger, no GST number and no network completes this in under ninety seconds. Everything else in the statement is scope for the finale, not for Thursday.

  1. New lot. One full-width button on an otherwise empty screen. Not a dashboard, not a login.
  2. Photograph the material. The photo is captured for the record first and for classification second — that ordering matters, and it is the reason the app works with no network.
  3. Confirm the category. An icon grid of six to eight scrap categories with Marathi labels, each spoken aloud on tap. The camera suggests; the collector decides. No free text anywhere in this flow.
  4. Enter the weight. Large numeric keypad, value spoken back in Marathi. This is where low-literacy design either works or is theatre.
  5. See the value, and who pays it. Rate × weight, large, spoken — then a ranked list of CPCB-authorised recyclers nearby with each one’s rate and distance. This single screen is the price transparency the whole thesis rests on.
  6. Produce the handover record. Photo, category, weight, agreed rate, total, timestamp, GPS, collector ID, recycler counter-signature. Rendered as a QR the recycler scans and a slip the collector keeps. The lot lands in the earnings ledger. This artefact is the product.

Why the handover record is the pitch, not a feature

For the collectorThe first receipt they have ever had. Proof of what they gave, to whom, at what rate.
For the recyclerAudit-grade evidence of physical tonnage, with photo, weight, time and place attached.
For the regulatorExactly the artefact a certificate market compromised by roughly 700,000 fake certificates is missing.
For your pitchIt is why the recycler can afford to pay more, which is why the collector earns more, which is the only reason anyone adopts this.

Most teams will build a marketplace and call the receipt a feature. Lead with the record and the economics close themselves.

Architecture

Nothing here requires a trained model, which is precisely why the statement suits this team. The hard engineering is offline sync; the visible craft is the low-literacy interface; the AI is supporting and should be described that way out loud.

The stack

  • Collector app — Flutter with Drift or sqflite, single APK, minimum SDK low enough for an entry-level device. Use whichever of Flutter or React Native someone on this team has already shipped. Do not learn a framework this week; a familiar stack is worth more than the right one.
  • Backend — FastAPI or Express with Postgres. Three endpoints is the whole surface: POST /sync, GET /recyclers, GET /rates. Anything beyond that is scope creep wearing a REST costume.
  • Recycler console — one web page. Scan the QR, see the lot, counter-sign, done. Next.js if you like, plain HTML against the same API if you are short on time. Judges will look at it for forty seconds.
  • Seed data — CPCB authorised-recycler lists are public, and the rates come from your own field visit. Load twenty real Nagpur-area recyclers, not two hundred fake ones.

The offline contract — the part that wins it

Offline-first is a stated requirement of the statement, and it is where a team that understands the user separates from a team that read the brief. Four rules, and they are also four good answers under questioning.

Local first
Every write commits to the local database and the UI advances immediately. Nothing in the collector flow ever awaits a network call. If a screen can show a spinner that depends on connectivity, it is built wrong.
Client IDs
Every record gets a UUID generated on the device, so no step needs a server round-trip to obtain an identity. This is what makes the whole chain work at zero bars.
Outbox
An append-only queue table with a synced_at column. Sync is a batch POST that the server upserts by UUID, so replaying it is harmless. Idempotent by construction, which is the correct answer when a judge asks about duplicates.
Immutable events
A handover record is a statement about something that happened, not mutable state, so it is append-only and conflict resolution is trivial. Corrections are new records that reference the old one. Say this in exactly these words if asked about sync conflicts — it demonstrates you chose the model rather than defaulted to it.

Photos compress to roughly 200 KB and upload separately and later. The record is valid before its photo has synced.

Where AI sits — and where it must not

  • Scrap classification is an accelerator, never a gate. Use an off-the-shelf hosted vision model to pre-select the category icon. If the call fails, times out, or there is no network, the icon grid is already on screen and the collector taps. In an offline-first app the classifier can never be on the critical path — state that as a design principle and it becomes a strength instead of a limitation.
  • Price estimation is a lookup table, seeded from your field visit and published rates, and it should stay one until real data justifies more. A team that ships a regression model over eleven observations has told the judges something about itself.
  • Fine-tuning is a promise, not a deliverable. Say plainly: the classifier is off-the-shelf today, and will be fine-tuned on the field photographs we are collecting. Honest scope beats an overclaim a judge can puncture.
  • LangGraph is a stretch goal with a hard gate. A bounded Marathi voice assistant over your own price and recycler data is a legitimate use of the one AI skill this team has — but it gets built only if the core loop is finished and demo-stable by Day 1 evening. Not before. If the gate is missed, it becomes a roadmap slide and costs you nothing.

Privacy, said out loud

The statement explicitly asks you to avoid unnecessary personal data, and most teams will skim past that line. Collect no Aadhaar, require no name, make the phone number optional, and give the collector a local pseudonymous ID. Then say so on a slide. Privacy is a scoring line most competitors leave blank, and this costs you one design decision and thirty seconds of stage time.

What you refuse to build

Write this list on the wall. Every hour lost to something on it is an hour not spent on the loop, the evidence or the number — and each of these is a perfectly reasonable idea that will still be reasonable in three weeks.

User accounts and login UPI and payments Push notifications Admin dashboard Model training Multi-city support In-app chat Ratings and reviews Route optimisation Blockchain anything

The trap this list exists to prevent

Six people over two days will produce roughly ninety working hours, and the natural failure mode is six parallel half-features rather than one finished path. A judge cannot distinguish a login screen you built from one you skipped, but they can absolutely tell whether the handover record actually generates. Depth on one path, zero width.

The deck

Use the official SIH idea-presentation template. Its sections have historically been Proposed Solution, Technical Approach, Feasibility and Viability, Impact and Benefits, and Research and References — Inference based on prior editions, so download the current one and confirm before you build. What follows is what belongs in each, for this statement.

Title
Product name, problem-statement code and title, team and institute. Nothing else. Ten seconds.
Proposed solution
The sentence from section 01, then the six-screen loop as six screenshots of your app. Not a diagram of a system, a picture of a person using it. Name the user precisely: an individual informal collector, cash, low literacy, entry-level Android, no GST number.
Technical approach
The offline contract — local-first writes, client-generated IDs, append-only outbox, immutable handover events. Then the stack in one line each. Then, explicitly, where AI is and is not: classification accelerates, it does not gate; pricing is a lookup, not a model. Judges reward a team that knows which parts do not need AI.
Feasibility and viability
The unit-economics slide — current ₹/kg against platform ₹/kg, one collector, one month, assumptions listed. Then the risks you already know: two-sided cold start, the credit hypothesis, Recykal extending downward. Naming your own risks before a judge does is the strongest move available on this slide.
Impact and benefits
1.39 million tonnes of e-waste in FY 2024-25 with roughly 95% handled informally; EPR collection targets rising to 80% by FY 2027-28; a certificate market with roughly 700,000 fake certificates documented. Then the local layer: the Nagpur study finding around 59% of surveyed scrap dealers already dismantling electronics, and Maharashtra’s four planned circular-economy parks, one in Nagpur.
Research and references
CPCB, the E-Waste Rules 2022, the CSE certificate findings, the Nagpur field study — and, if the reply has arrived, JNARDDC quoted directly. A quoted sponsor on the references slide changes how the whole deck reads.

What the official rubric actually weights

Five criteria at ten marks each, fifty total — Innovation and Originality, Feasibility and Realistic Implementation, Social and Environmental Impact or Business Value, Technical Execution, and Presentation. Twenty per cent apiece. Fact

The circulating claim that presentation is now worth around half the marks is community intelligence rather than published policy. Treat it as a reason to over-invest in the pitch, never as permission to under-build: on the official sheet, Technical Execution and Feasibility together outweigh Presentation two to one.

The four-minute demo

Run it in this order every time, live, with the recorded video ready on a second device. The airplane-mode moment is the whole demo — everything before it is setup and everything after it is consequence.

  1. Start in airplane mode. Say so. Turn it on in front of the room before you touch the app. “This phone has no network for the next three minutes.” Proving offline works beats asserting it, and the claim is unfalsifiable if you assert it from a connected device.
  2. Photograph real scrap. Bring actual material to the room if you can — a handful of cables and a circuit board in a bag costs nothing and changes the temperature of the presentation entirely.
  3. Tap the category, enter the weight, hear the Marathi. Let the spoken value play out loud. The room hears the accessibility argument rather than reading it on a slide.
  4. Show the value and the ranked recyclers, and name one of them as a real CPCB-authorised recycler you loaded from public data. Specificity is the credibility.
  5. Generate the handover record. Hand the phone to a teammate playing the recycler, who scans the QR on the console and counter-signs. The loop closes in front of the judges.
  6. Now turn airplane mode off, and let the outbox sync while you talk. The records appear on the console. That is the last thing they see, and it is the thing they will remember.

Rules for the live demo

One phone, charged, on airplane mode, with the app already installed and the flow already tested on that device. No screen mirroring you have not tested in that room. No live API calls to anything you do not control. If the demo fails, do not debug in front of the panel — say “here it is running from this morning”, play the video, and carry on. A team with a backup looks prepared; a team debugging looks finished.

The question bank

Every answer in one or two sentences. If someone needs a paragraph, they do not know it yet. The first four are the ones that decide the round.

Recykal already does this. Why do you exist?
Recykal.Market onboards aggregators — businesses with a smartphone, a ledger and a GST number. We onboard the person below them, who has none of those. Then show the collector saying it in their own words on the field video. That is the moment the room decides, so rehearse this one until it is reflex.
Why would a collector actually switch?
Because the authorised recycler can pay more for material that arrives with a verifiable handover record, and we share that difference at the point of sale. Then quote the rate you collected in the field. If your interview said the collector is tied to their buyer by credit rather than by price, say that instead — naming the constraint you discovered is far stronger than pretending you did not.
Two-sided marketplace — who joins first?
One material stream, one city, one recycler. We start with PCBs and cables in Nagpur and a single authorised recycler who wants auditable tonnage, and we recruit collectors against that one guaranteed buyer. We are not launching a marketplace, we are closing one route.
Where is the AI? This looks like a CRUD app.
Classification suggests the category from the photograph, and it is deliberately not on the critical path, because the app has to work with no network. The genuinely hard engineering here is offline sync and the audit record — and a team that adds a model where a lookup table works has built the wrong product. Say this confidently; it is a better answer than a model demo.
How do you know the weight and the photo are not faked?
We do not, on the collector side alone — which is exactly why the record requires a recycler counter-signature and carries timestamp and GPS. It is not a trust system, it is a two-party evidence system, and the recycler has a compliance reason to be honest.
Who pays for it?
The recycler and, through them, the producer — both need audit-grade physical tonnage, and both currently pay for certificates rather than evidence. The collector never pays anything, and making them pay would end the product.
The name is already taken.
Correct — Kabadiwalla Connect is a Chennai enterprise operating since 2014. We kept the problem-statement title in the submission and named the product separately. Volunteer this before a judge raises it.
Why should the ministry adopt this?
SIH’s own project-implementation guidelines ask the ministry to name a technical agency under its aegis to run implementation. Here that agency already exists, it is JNARDDC, it wrote this statement, and it is in Nagpur — and here is what they told us when we wrote to them.
What about data protection?
The statement asks us to avoid unnecessary personal data, so we collect none — no Aadhaar, no name required, an optional phone number and a local pseudonymous collector ID. The handover record contains material, weight, time and place, not identity.
Does this work outside Nagpur?
The software is language-configurable and the recycler list is data, so the answer is yes — but we would rather prove one city than claim twenty. The defensible asset is the collector relationships and the price dataset, and both compound per city rather than transferring for free.
What if there is no network for a week?
Nothing breaks. Records queue locally with device-generated IDs and sync as a batch when connectivity returns, and because the records are immutable events the replay is idempotent. We tested it with the outbox full.
What did you actually build, and what is roadmap?
Answer this one with complete precision — the loop end to end offline with a real recycler list and real field rates is built; the fine-tuned classifier, the voice assistant and payments are roadmap. Teams lose more credibility to a vague claim than to a short honest list.

If it breaks

Three failures are plausible enough to have a decided answer in advance, so that the decision is made now rather than at two in the morning by whoever is most tired.

The field visit cannot happen

This is the serious one, because the interview is the advantage — without it you are pitching the same statement as everyone else with none of the evidence. If it genuinely cannot happen in the window, switch to SIH26034, Legal Metrology compliance scanning: its field research is thirty photographs in a supermarket, doable in twenty minutes at any hour, and its build is the lowest-risk in the top ten. A statement you can evidence beats a better statement you cannot.

The collector says they are tied to their buyer by credit

Then the core assumption is wrong in its simple form, and you found that out in a two-hour visit that most teams will not make. Do not bury it. Put it on the feasibility slide as the primary risk, and adjust the pitch: the handover record and the recycler-side value still stand on their own, and the price-transparency claim becomes conditional rather than central. A team that discovered and named the flaw looks stronger than a team that never looked.

Gate 2 fails and the loop is not working by Day 1 evening

Cut the recycler console to a static mockup, cut the classifier entirely so the icon grid is the only input, and finish the collector path plus the handover record. Those two are the demo. A complete short loop with a generated record beats a longer flow that stalls on stage, and nobody in the room will miss what you removed.

The three things that must be true at 19:00 on Day 2

One loop that works offline on a real phone. One piece of evidence nobody else has — a Marathi recording of a Nagpur collector, and their real rates in your app. One number — current ₹/kg against platform ₹/kg, with the assumptions written down.

Everything in this document exists to protect those three. If an hour on Day 2 is not defending one of them, it is being spent wrong.
